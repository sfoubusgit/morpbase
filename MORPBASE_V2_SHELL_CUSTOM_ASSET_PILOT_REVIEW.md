# MorpBase V2 Shell Custom Asset Pilot Review

## Verdict

- **Pass**

## What Improved

The top of V2 now feels more like:

- one authored product frame

and less like:

- a well-designed shell that is still waiting for custom identity support

### 1. The top-frame now has clearer join language

The new shell-join fragments help the frame feel:

- held
- intentional
- and more singular

without making it feel:

- bordered for its own sake

### 2. The brand anchor now feels more embedded

The new brand support fragment is small, but it helps the brand block feel more like:

- part of the frame

and less like:

- a neat label sitting inside it

### 3. The engine-strip join now feels more authored

The new join fragments help the knot feel more like:

- a real shell-to-engine handoff

and less like:

- a nice divider between two good sections

### 4. The primary nav now carries a little more structural weight

The subtle group cue helps the main realm cluster feel more like:

- the true working center of the product

without turning it into:

- a louder nav treatment

## What This Pass Did Not Break

Important:

- the shell did not become ornamental
- the frame did not get heavier
- the shell still reads as one world, not a luxury skin

## Recommended Next Move

- **threshold custom asset pilot**

Why:

- shell was the biggest whole-product identity gain
- thresholds are already the clearest proven symbolic family
- and they are the strongest next place for truly custom refinement

## Honest Conclusion

This was the right first custom asset pilot because it made MorpBase feel more authored at the whole-product level without reopening product structure or drifting into visual theater.
