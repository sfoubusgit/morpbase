# MorpBase V2 Design-Universe Preparation Prompt

You are analyzing **MorpBase V2** as a **completely new rebuilt product in a new project folder**, not as a refinement of V1.

Your task is to create the **Design-Universe Preparation** for V2 first-wave.

This phase comes **after**:

- foundation freeze
- product blueprint
- interaction blueprint
- terminology and micro-language freeze

This is not yet final UI design.
It is the phase where the product gets its **visual and atmospheric direction brief**.

The goal is to define the kind of visual universe MorpBase V2 should grow into so that later design/prototype work does not fall back into generic app design.

## Core Context

The product now has a stable structural and language foundation.

Important strategic constraint:

- MorpBase V2 should feel like a **real product**
- and it should **not** have a generic design
- it should eventually live inside a **specific, unique design universe**
- Adobe Illustrator may be used later to build custom assets for that universe

The design-universe preparation must therefore answer:

- what kind of product atmosphere V2 should have
- how hierarchy should be expressed visually
- what should make MorpBase feel distinctive
- what visual language principles should guide later design
- where custom illustration/iconography/shape systems may matter

## Critical Constraints

- Treat V2 as a **fresh rebuild**
- Obey the foundation freeze, product blueprint, interaction blueprint, and terminology freeze
- Do **not** design generic SaaS UI
- Do **not** jump straight into colors, fonts, and components without defining the design logic first
- Do **not** let visual ambition erase product clarity
- Do **not** make the design universe purely decorative; it must support hierarchy, identity, and usability

## What This Preparation Must Cover

At minimum, it must address:

### 1. Product atmosphere

- what V2 should feel like emotionally and visually

### 2. Hierarchy expression

- how `Workspace`, `Memory`, `Community`, and `Continuity` should differ in weight and feel

### 3. Visual identity logic

- what kinds of visual signals should make MorpBase recognizable
- what should remain calm vs expressive

### 4. Surface family logic

- how the main screen families should feel related but not identical

### 5. Custom asset opportunities

- where custom Illustrator-built assets might matter most:
  - shell
  - icons
  - illustrations
  - object cards
  - continuity visuals
  - public/community signals

### 6. Anti-generic rules

- what the future design must avoid to stay product-distinct

## Method

### 1. Start with real-life visual/product moments first

Write at least 5 realistic moments such as:

- first-time user enters Workspace and immediately senses “this is not another generic AI tool”
- user moves from Workspace to Memory and feels a shift in mode without feeling in a different app
- user enters Community and feels public energy without feed chaos
- user enters Continuity and feels it is special but not overblown
- the shell feels premium and distinct without becoming heavy

For each moment, explain what visual/product-design demands it creates.

### 2. Generate multiple design-universe directions

Create at least 5 genuinely different visual/product-direction candidates, such as:

- precision atelier / crafted studio
- luminous systems / subtle ritual-tech
- archival-modern creative instrument
- editorial creative lab
- symbolic continuity-forward world

You may create stronger alternatives if needed.

For each candidate, explain:

- what kind of product it would make MorpBase feel like
- what it gets right
- where it risks harming clarity or product fit

### 3. Compare the candidates seriously

Explicitly judge:

- fit with the V2 product hierarchy
- distinctiveness
- usability support
- compatibility with the language system
- ability to support a custom Illustrator-based asset universe later
- resistance to generic AI-tool design
- resistance to ornamental overdesign

### 4. Choose the strongest design-universe direction or synthesis

Lock the strongest direction for MorpBase V2.

### 5. Make the result concrete

Define:

- the design-universe direction
- the emotional/product keywords
- the hierarchy expression rules
- the surface-family rules
- where custom asset systems matter most
- what must stay calm vs expressive
- what future UI design must not violate

## Output Requirements

Write the result as:

`MORPBASE_V2_DESIGN_UNIVERSE_PREPARATION.md`

Structure it with:

1. Executive Conclusion
2. Real-Life Visual/Product Moments
3. Candidate Design-Universe Directions
4. Comparison
5. Chosen Design-Universe Direction
6. Hierarchy Expression Rules
7. Surface Family Rules
8. Custom Asset Opportunities
9. Anti-Generic Design Rules
10. Readiness For Experience Prototyping
11. Final Judgment

The result should be strategic, product-design-oriented, and strong enough to guide later V2 visual design and prototyping work.
