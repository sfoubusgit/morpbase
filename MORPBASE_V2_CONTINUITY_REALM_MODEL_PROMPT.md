# MorpBase V2 Continuity Realm Model Prompt

You are analyzing **MorpBase V2** as a **completely new rebuilt product**, not as a refinement of V1.

Your task is to determine the strongest **Continuity realm model** for V2.

This is not just an `Identity Systems` exercise.

You must decide what `Continuity` actually is as a V2 product realm:

- what it fundamentally owns
- how it differs from `Memory`
- how it differs from the live `Workspace`
- how it differs from `Community`
- how it relates to `Identity Systems`
- and what first-wave Continuity should look like without over-staging a long-range vision

The already-established V2 whole-product hierarchy is:

1. live workflow center
2. product memory
3. continuity
4. community / ecosystem
5. long-range horizon

Known continuity-adjacent truths already established:

- continuity entities are reusable entities that should remain recognizably the same across workflows
- continuity contributes lineage into saved outputs
- continuity is visible in first-wave, but lighter than `Workspace`, `Memory`, and `Community`
- `Identity Systems` has earlier been explored as a future realm for reusable continuity entities
- `Shared Storylines` and larger continuity structures are long-range horizon, not first-wave

Your job is to determine:

- what the Continuity realm fundamentally is
- what first-wave Continuity should contain
- how much of it should be entity-focused vs lineage-focused
- whether it should feel like a library, an atlas, a manager, a workspace, or something else
- how it should preserve headroom for later story/world continuity without pretending that V2 first-wave already has that maturity

## Critical Constraints

- Treat V2 as a **fresh rebuild**
- Do **not** assume `Continuity = Identity Systems`
- Do **not** make Continuity another `Memory` subsection
- Do **not** make Continuity a second workspace
- Do **not** make Continuity a vague ambition layer with no concrete ownership
- Do **not** overbuild narrative/world systems too early
- Do **not** let first-wave Continuity become product theater

## Method

### 1. Start with real-life usage drafts first

Write at least 5 realistic continuity journeys such as:

- user wants a recurring character/entity to remain stable across multiple workflows
- user wants to understand where a continuity entity has appeared before
- user wants to activate continuity in Workspace without defining the entity there
- user wants to browse recurring continuity-linked work through Memory
- later storyline/world continuity grows and must fit above first-wave continuity without replacing it

For each draft, explain what structural demands it creates.

### 2. Generate multiple Continuity realm candidates

Create at least 5 genuinely different Continuity models, such as:

- identity-library model
- lineage/archive model
- continuity atlas model
- worldbuilding/storyline realm
- contextual side-realm with no serious home

You may create stronger alternatives if needed.

For each candidate, explain:

- what the realm fundamentally is
- what it emphasizes
- what it gets right
- where it fails

### 3. Compare the candidates seriously

Explicitly judge:

- clarity
- distinct ownership
- fit with V2 whole-product hierarchy
- relationship to Identity Systems
- usefulness in first-wave
- resistance to overbuilding
- headroom for later continuity growth

### 4. Choose the strongest model or synthesis

Lock the best Continuity realm model for MorpBase V2.

### 5. Make the result concrete

Define:

- what Continuity fundamentally is
- its first-wave internal structure
- what is always visible
- what is lane-based
- what belongs later
- how it connects to Workspace, Memory, and Community

## Output Requirements

Write the result as:

`MORPBASE_V2_CONTINUITY_REALM_MODEL_ANALYSIS.md`

Structure it with:

1. Executive Conclusion
2. Real-Life Usage Drafts
3. Candidate Continuity Realm Models
4. Comparison
5. Chosen Continuity Realm Model
6. Recommended First-Wave Structure
7. Relationship To Identity, Memory, Workspace, And Community
8. First-Wave vs Later Growth
9. Guardrails
10. Final Judgment

The result should be decisive, product-structural, and willing to correct earlier continuity assumptions if they do not survive whole-product scrutiny.
