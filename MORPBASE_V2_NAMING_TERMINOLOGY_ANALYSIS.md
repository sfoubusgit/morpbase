# MorpBase V2 Naming And Terminology Analysis

## 1. Executive Conclusion

The strongest naming system for MorpBase V2 is:

- **clear top-level product language with selective signature depth underneath**

This means V2 should not use:

- heavy invented jargon at the top level
- or fully generic product language everywhere

Instead, it should use:

- **clear, legible, product-real names for the main realms**
- **clear object names for the most repeated product objects**
- **more selective, earned signature language only where it adds real identity**

The key judgment is:

- V2 should sound like a real product first
- and a distinctive MorpBase second
- not the other way around

So the winning naming system is:

- plain enough to understand fast
- distinctive enough to feel like MorpBase
- disciplined enough to keep internal architecture mostly backstage

## 2. Real-Life Naming Drafts

### Draft 1. First-time user sees the shell and interprets the realm names

Goal:

- understand the product structure without needing onboarding copy to decode it

What this demands:

- top-level realm names must be immediately legible
- they cannot rely on internal lore or product-specific jargon
- they must support the asymmetrical shell hierarchy naturally

### Draft 2. User saves work and tries to understand what `Memory` means

Goal:

- feel that this is more than an archive without being confused

What this demands:

- some realm names can be slightly expressive if they still clarify product meaning
- `Memory` works only if surrounding object names make its role concrete

### Draft 3. User enters `Continuity` and needs to understand it without a lecture

Goal:

- grasp that this realm is about recurring sameness across work

What this demands:

- the realm name can be slightly more conceptual than `Workspace` or `Community`
- but the internal object names inside it must stay concrete

### Draft 4. Creator uses public/community surfaces and needs language that feels real

Goal:

- feel that public work and reuse live in a serious product layer

What this demands:

- public object names must feel actionable and credible
- not abstract, not too technical, and not decorative

### Draft 5. Advanced user senses product depth without being buried in system jargon

Goal:

- feel the product has richness without becoming hard to learn

What this demands:

- some depth should come from structure and use, not from noun count
- advanced/internal distinctions should not all become visible terms

These drafts strongly suggest:

- top-level names should be clear and stable
- deeper language should be controlled carefully
- and internal/product-facing naming must be separated explicitly

## 3. Candidate Naming Systems

### Candidate A. Fully Plain Literal System

Philosophy:

- use literal everyday product words almost everywhere

What it makes MorpBase feel like:

- very clear
- very approachable
- but somewhat generic

What it gets right:

- high comprehension
- low jargon

Where it fails:

- loses too much distinctiveness
- risks making MorpBase sound like any other productivity or creative tool

### Candidate B. Branded / Mythic Naming System

Philosophy:

- give most major systems proprietary or symbolic names

What it makes MorpBase feel like:

- highly distinctive
- potentially lore-rich

What it gets right:

- memorable if done very well
- can support a strong design universe later

Where it fails:

- too risky for first-use clarity
- too close to V1's noun-burden problem
- makes product meaning harder to read

### Candidate C. Generic Modern SaaS System

Philosophy:

- use current product-default terms everywhere

What it makes MorpBase feel like:

- polished
- familiar

What it gets right:

- easy to scan
- low explanation cost

Where it fails:

- too bland
- too interchangeable
- weak product identity

### Candidate D. Mixed Product-Plain System

Philosophy:

- use clear product words at the top
- allow slightly more expressive names in selected deeper areas

What it makes MorpBase feel like:

- readable
- product-shaped
- somewhat distinctive

What it gets right:

- good first-use clarity
- some room for identity

Where it fails:

- can become inconsistent if the naming logic is not disciplined

### Candidate E. Layered Signature System

Philosophy:

- top level stays clear and legible
- core repeated objects stay concrete
- selective sub-language and later branded/visual identity supply distinctiveness
- internal architecture terms remain backstage

What it makes MorpBase feel like:

- clear
- intentional
- product-real
- distinct without sounding overnamed

What it gets right:

- best balance of clarity and identity
- strongest fit for first-wave V2
- strongest resistance to both genericity and jargon overload

Where it fails:

- requires discipline so "signature language" stays selective

## 4. Comparison

### Why Candidate A loses

Because it solves clarity by sacrificing too much identity.

### Why Candidate B loses

Because it tries to solve identity through terminology volume, which is one of the things V2 is explicitly trying to escape.

### Why Candidate C loses

Because it makes MorpBase sound too generic and interchangeable.

### Why Candidate D is close but loses

Because it is directionally right, but not disciplined enough as a full naming philosophy.

### Why Candidate E wins

Because it gives MorpBase:

- clear top-level comprehension
- enough distinctive product language
- strong resistance to V1 noun overload
- and room for a future custom visual/design universe to carry more of the brand identity

So the strongest result is:

- **a layered signature system**

## 5. Chosen Naming Philosophy

The chosen naming philosophy is:

- **clear top-level product language with selective signature depth underneath**

In practice, this means:

### 1. Top-level names must be highly legible

The shell and main product map should use names users can understand quickly.

### 2. Repeated object names must be concrete

The most-used objects should have names that describe what they are for.

### 3. Signature language should be earned, not scattered

V2 should not try to make every system sound special.

### 4. Internal architecture terms stay backstage

Not every structural distinction deserves a public noun.

### 5. Distinctiveness should also come from design, interaction, and product shape

Not just from unusual terminology.

## 6. Recommended Top-Level Names

The strongest current top-level names are:

- `Workspace`
- `Memory`
- `Community`
- `Continuity`
- `Profile`

Judgment by term:

### Workspace

Keep.

Why:

- clear
- active
- fits the dominant center

### Memory

Keep.

Why:

- more alive than `Archive`
- more product-real than `Library`
- expresses the second-pole role well

### Community

Keep.

Why:

- clear
- credible
- broad enough to support public circulation and later vitality

### Continuity

Keep, with caution.

Why:

- conceptually right
- stronger than `Identity`
- broader than `Characters`
- fits the realm model

But:

- the internal language inside Continuity must stay concrete so the realm does not become abstract

### Profile

Keep.

Why:

- utility-clear
- avoids inflating account surfaces into a product realm

## 7. Recommended Object / Surface Naming

These are the strongest current object/surface naming directions:

### Main center

- `Workspace`
- `Prompt Preview`

`Prompt Preview` should likely stay unless later visual/product design suggests a better paired name.

Why:

- it is accurate
- already clear
- and the surrounding product can provide distinctiveness

### Memory

Strong section names:

- `Memory Home`
- `Saved Work`
- `Reusable Assets`
- `Capture Inbox`

Why these work:

- they are concrete
- they make `Memory` easier to understand
- they avoid over-poetic storage language

### Community

Strong section names:

- `Discover`
- `Creators`
- `Publishing`

Why these work:

- they map to real user intentions
- they keep Community practical
- they avoid market/feed/platform jargon

### Continuity

Strong first-wave section names:

- `Continuity Home`
- `Entities`
- `Appearances`

Potential note:

- `Lineage / Appearances` should probably simplify to one stronger user-facing label later.
- Right now, `Appearances` is likely the stronger user-facing option, with `lineage` staying more backstage or explanatory.

### Public object names

For internal / structural use:

- `Public Workflow Results`
- `Public Reusable Assets`

For likely user-facing shorthand:

- `Published Work`
- `Reusable Assets`

Why:

- `Public Workflow Results` is structurally accurate but slightly heavy as repeated surface language
- `Published Work` is cleaner for repeated public-facing use
- `Reusable Assets` is already clear enough to survive in public-facing language

### Continuity entities

For first-wave:

- `Characters`

Potential later lanes:

- `Outfits`
- `Artifacts`
- `Creatures`

Why:

- lane names should be concrete and plural
- not hidden behind framework language

### Repeated action language

Prefer:

- `Keep`
- `Save`
- `Open`
- `Use`
- `Import`
- `Publish`
- `Follow`
- `Branch`

Avoid:

- overly system-specific verbs
- abstract verbs that need product explanation

## 8. Backstage / Internal Naming Rules

These should remain mostly backstage or explanatory, not front-stage product nouns:

- `active workflow session`
- `shared creative objects`
- `continuity entities`
- `lineage semantics`
- `workflow-shaping assets`
- other architecture-accurate but user-heavy phrases

Rule:

- if a term mainly helps design, architecture, or product analysis, it should not automatically become a user-facing noun

Another rule:

- if a distinction can be expressed through layout, grouping, or action instead of vocabulary, prefer that

## 9. Terms To Retire From V1

These should be retired from the main V2 product language unless a later very strong reason re-emerges:

- `Working Sets`
- `Pools` as a front-stage noun
- `Territories` as a front-stage noun
- `IDP Sets` as a front-stage noun
- `Initiative Phrases` as a front-stage noun
- `Prompt Sets` as a major front-stage noun

Why:

- they are either too legacy-shaped
- too internal
- too cognitively expensive
- or too specific to survive V2 unchanged

Important nuance:

- the functions behind some of these may survive
- but the names should not be preserved automatically

## 10. Final Judgment

The strongest naming system for MorpBase V2 is:

- **a layered signature terminology system with clear top-level product language, concrete repeated object names, selective deeper identity, and strict backstage discipline for internal concepts**

That is the cleanest way to make V2:

- more understandable than V1
- more product-real than V1
- less generic than default SaaS language
- and more capable of carrying a future custom visual/design universe without overloading terminology.
